## What This Source Covers Well

- continuous symmetries and conservation laws
- Lagrangian mechanics, field Lagrangians, and Noether's theorem
- group theory basics, Lie generators, and the Lorentz group
- special-relativistic geometry, four-vectors, and index notation
- relativistic dynamics, four-momentum, four-force, proper acceleration, rapidity, and collisions
- covariant electromagnetism: four-current, four-potential, gauge transformations, electromagnetic tensor, Maxwell equations, and Lorentz force
- electromagnetic stress-energy tensors
- radiation from moving charges, including retarded potentials, Lienard-Wiechert potentials, Larmor's formula, dipole radiation, scattering, antenna radiation, and the Lienard formula

## Structure And Style

The notes move in six large blocks:

1. symmetries, Lagrangian methods, field theory, and Noether's theorem
2. groups, representations, Lie algebras, and Lorentz transformations
3. special-relativistic geometry and four-vector calculus
4. relativistic dynamics and particle processes
5. special relativity applied to electromagnetism
6. radiation from moving charges

The later chapters are dense in index notation. For questions about electromagnetism or stress-energy, prefer targeted TeX reads from the locator rather than broad top-down reading.

## Notation And Conventions

- Uses contravariant and covariant four-vector components extensively from Chapter 3 onward.
- Electromagnetism is written covariantly in terms of $J^\mu$, $A^\mu$, and $F^{\mu\nu}$.
- The symmetric electromagnetic stress-energy tensor is the preferred physical tensor after the canonical tensor is introduced.
