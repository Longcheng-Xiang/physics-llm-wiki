## Ewart TeX — Quantum Defect Definition (lines 576–596)

> As noted already, the single outer electron in an alkali moves in a potential that is "central" to an excellent approximation. ... The degree of core penetration depends on $l$ and very little on $n$. As a result the deviation from the hydrogenic energy level is almost constant for a given $l$ as $n$ increases.
>
> The hydrogen energy levels can be expressed as
> $$E_n = -\frac{R}{n^2}$$
> For alkalis ... the excited state energies may be expressed as
> $$E_n = -\frac{R}{(n^*)^2}$$
> Where $n^*$ is an effective quantum number. $n^*$ differs from the equivalent $n$-value in hydrogen by $\delta(l)$, i.e. $n^* = n - \delta(l)$.
> $\delta(l)$ is the quantum defect and depends largely on $l$ only. It is found empirically, and it can be shown theoretically, to be independent of $n$.

## Ewart TeX — Series Identification Procedure (lines 608–635)

> We also need to remember that a transition involves a change in $l$ of $\pm 1$ ($\Delta l = \pm 1$). ... For simplicity ... the ground state has $l=0$ then there will be only a single set of transitions to levels with angular momentum one unit larger i.e. $l=1$.
>
> (1) Measure the wavelength of each absorption line in the series, $\lambda_n$.
> (2) Calculate the corresponding wavenumber $\bar{\nu}_n = \lambda_n^{-1}$
> (3) Estimate the value of the series limit $\bar{\nu}_\infty$. This corresponds to the ionisation potential or Term value for the ground state, $T_{n_0}$.
> (4) Find the Term value, $T_n$, or binding energy of an excited level from the difference:
> $$T_{n_i} = T_{n_0} - \bar{\nu}_n$$
> (5) The Term value is given by the hydrogenic formula:
> $$T_{n_i} = \frac{R_\infty}{(n_i^*)^2}, \qquad n_i^* = n - \delta(l)$$

## Ewart TeX — Selection Rules (lines 1682–1691)

> The angular momentum does not depend at all on $n$, so $n$ can change by anything.
> Our basic rules are therefore:
> $$\Delta n = \text{anything}, \qquad \Delta l = \pm 1$$
> And the photon carries one unit of angular momentum.
